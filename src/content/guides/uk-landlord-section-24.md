## Who this is for

This is for individual UK landlords (and partners in a property partnership) who need to track rental income and costs for HMRC Self Assessment — often using the **UK property** pages (SA105).

You want **Google Sheets**, GBP, and UK tax years (6 April to 5 April), not a US Schedule E template.

Company landlords and complex structures have different rules. This guide focuses on the everyday sole landlord / individual property business who needs tidy books.

If you also have a side hustle, keep property income separate from self-employment (SA103) records — different forms, different boxes.

---

## What to record for rental Self Assessment

GOV.UK expects accurate records of rent and expenses so you can work out taxable profit and show evidence if asked. Useful starting points:

- [Work out your rental income](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/income-tax-when-you-rent-out-a-property-working-out-your-rental-income)
- [Paying tax when you rent out a property](https://www.gov.uk/renting-out-a-property/paying-tax)
- [Self Assessment: UK property (SA105)](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/self-assessment-uk-property-sa105)

Plan to track at least:

- **Rent and other property income** — amounts and dates (gross rent before agent fees if that is how you reconcile statements)
- **Allowable expenses** — costs wholly and exclusively for the letting (see GOV.UK’s list: repairs, insurance, agent fees, utilities you pay, and similar)
- **Dates and property** — which flat or house each line belongs to
- **Proof** — invoices, agent statements, bank statements, rent books

Keep records for the period GOV.UK requires (for property income this is commonly **at least 5 years after the 31 January deadline** for the relevant tax year — confirm on the live guidance).

Whether you must register for Self Assessment, and when the **property allowance** applies, depends on your figures and situation. Use GOV.UK’s current thresholds rather than memorising a blog post.

## Section 24 in plain English (finance cost restriction)

People often call the residential landlord finance-cost rules **“Section 24”** (from Finance (No. 2) Act 2015). In everyday terms:

For many **individual** residential landlords, **mortgage interest and similar finance costs are no longer deducted in full** when you calculate rental *profit*. Instead, relief is generally given as a **basic-rate tax reduction** after profits are worked out.

Official explanations (read these — do not rely on this summary alone):

- [Tax relief for residential landlords: how it's worked out](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/changes-to-tax-relief-for-residential-landlords-how-its-worked-out-including-case-studies)
- [Changes to tax relief for residential landlords](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/changes-to-tax-relief-for-residential-landlords)

What that means for your spreadsheet:

- Still **record** mortgage interest and other finance costs carefully (date, amount, property, lender)
- Keep them **clearly labelled** as finance costs, separate from repairs and agent fees
- Do **not** assume your sheet’s “profit” line is the same as your taxable property profit after the restriction — your accountant or the SA105 notes will place finance costs in the right boxes
- Commercial property and companies can be treated differently — check GOV.UK if that is you

This article does **not** invent or guarantee a tax rate for your case. Basic-rate relief and transitional history are explained on GOV.UK; confirm the current position for the tax year you are filing.

## Why generic US landlord sheets fail in the UK

US and “global” landlord templates often:

- Use a calendar year instead of the UK tax year
- Mix Schedule E categories that do not map to SA105
- Treat mortgage interest like a simple expense deduction every year
- Ignore UK agent statement formats and GBP
- Assume Excel desktop files

A UK landlord tracker should speak **tax year, SA105-style income and expenses, and labelled finance costs** — and run in Google Sheets if that is how you work.

## Minimum columns for a landlord sheet

A practical ledger (one row per transaction):

1. **Date**
2. **Property** (address or short label)
3. **Type** — Income / Expense / Finance cost
4. **Category** — e.g. rent, agent fee, repair, insurance, ground rent, mortgage interest
5. **Description**
6. **Amount (GBP)**
7. **Tax year** (e.g. 2025/26)
8. **Notes / invoice or statement ref**

Optional: tenant or tenancy ID, payment method, “reconciled to agent statement?”

**Worked mini-example (fictional only):**

| Date | Property | Type | Category | Amount (£) | Tax year |

| 15 May 2025 | Flat A | Finance cost | Mortgage interest | 420.00 | 2025/26 |

At year end, filter by tax year and property, total income, total allowable expenses, and total finance costs separately — then take those summaries to your return or accountant.

## FAQ

### Do I need Self Assessment as a landlord?

Often yes once rental income or profits cross the levels GOV.UK sets for reporting, or if HMRC asks you to file. Check [paying tax when you rent out a property](https://www.gov.uk/renting-out-a-property/paying-tax) and register guidance for your year.

### Can I put mortgage interest in with repairs?

Keep finance costs separate. Under the residential finance-cost restriction, they are usually treated differently from day-to-day allowable expenses. See GOV.UK’s landlord tax-relief pages above.

### Is Section 24 the same for limited companies?

The restriction described for individuals does not work the same way for companies. If you let through a company, get advice and use the guidance that matches your structure.
