## Who this is for

This is for UK households — solo or sharing — who know December gets expensive and January feels worse.

You want a simple **Google Sheets** plan: who you are buying for, what you can spend, how much you have saved so far, and what bills will still land after the holidays.

No shame spiral. Just a clear list and a weekly transfer.

## Why start before December

Starting in September, October, or early November gives you more paydays to spread the cost. A smaller weekly amount beats a single panic shop in mid-December on a credit card you are still paying in March.

Early planning also surfaces the quiet costs people forget: wrapping, postage, school fairs, office Secret Santa, travel home, food shop uplift, and New Year plans.

January is when many UK bills feel heavy at once — rent or mortgage, energy, and for some households the return of **council tax** after the February/March gap if you pay over 10 months. A Christmas tracker that ignores January is only half a plan.

## Gift list columns that actually help

Keep one row per person or per cost. Suggested columns:

1. **Person / item** (Mum, teacher gift, tree, travel)
2. **Category** — gift / food / travel / decorations / charity / other
3. **Idea**
4. **Budget (£)**
5. **Actual spent (£)**
6. **Bought?** (yes/no)
7. **Notes** (size, link, shared gift with sibling)

Optional: “from whose pot” if partners split categories, and a hard **household Christmas cap** at the top of the sheet.

Total the **Budget** column first. If the sum scares you, cut ideas before you cut savings. Then track **Actual** so you see drift early — not on Christmas Eve.

## A sinking fund for January bills

Treat January as its own line, not an afterthought.

Common UK January pressures:

- Rent or mortgage (same as every month — but cash is tighter after December)
- Energy bills (winter usage)
- Council tax (if your instalments restart or you feel the gap after the 10-month cycle)
- Credit card balances from December
- Subscriptions and annual renewals that hit in January

Pick a round **January buffer** target (even £100–£200 helps; larger if you know January is brutal). Divide by the weeks left until Christmas or until 1 January. Transfer that amount to a named savings pot or a labelled row on the sheet every payday.

Gifts and January buffer compete for the same money. Decide the split on purpose: for example 70% gifts/food/travel, 30% January — then adjust once, not every time you see a sale.

---

## Weekly rhythm from autumn

Pick a 15-minute slot each week (Sunday evening works for many):

1. Add anyone you forgot to the gift list
2. Update **Actual spent** from your banking app
3. Move this week’s Christmas + January transfers
4. Check remaining budget vs weeks left
5. If you are over on gifts, cut one idea or reduce a flexible category — do not raid the January buffer first

From early December, freeze new “nice to have” names on the list unless something comes off.

## FAQ

### When should I start a Christmas savings tracker?

As soon as you can — ideally by early autumn. Starting in November still helps; starting on 1 December is better than not tracking at all.

### Should Christmas go on the credit card?

Only if you already have a written payoff plan inside your January buffer. If December spend becomes unplanned revolving debt, the tracker’s job is to shrink the list, not to justify the card.

### Can I share the sheet with a partner?

Yes — Google Sheets is built for one live file. Agree a total cap and who buys what so you do not double-buy.

### Is this financial advice?

No — organisation and planning ideas only.
