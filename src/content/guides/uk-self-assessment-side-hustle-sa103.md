## Who this is for

This is for people in the UK with a side hustle or freelance income they earn as a sole trader — tutoring, Etsy selling, content creation, weekend trades, consulting on the side, and similar.

You usually need to think about Self Assessment when you have self-employment income. GOV.UK currently says you must send a tax return if, in the last tax year, you were self-employed as a sole trader and earned more than £1,000 (before reliefs). Other situations can also trigger a return. Confirm against the live rules:

- [Who must send a tax return](https://www.gov.uk/self-assessment-tax-returns/who-must-send-a-tax-return)
- [Check how to register for Self Assessment](https://www.gov.uk/register-for-self-assessment)

If you are unsure, use HMRC’s checker on GOV.UK rather than guessing.

**SA103** is the self-employment section of the Self Assessment return (the full version is often called SA103F). Your spreadsheet is not the form itself. It is the working file that holds the figures you need when you complete those pages.

## What HMRC expects you to keep

If you are self-employed, HMRC expects you to keep records of business income and expenses so you can complete an accurate return and show evidence if asked.

At a minimum, plan to track:

- All sales and income (what you earned, from whom or which platform, and when)
- All business expenses (what you spent, on what, and when)
- Supporting proof (invoices, receipts, platform statements, bank statements)

Digital records are fine if they are accurate and clear. You do not normally send the full paperwork with your return, but you must keep it.

How long to keep records is set out on GOV.UK. For self-employed people, the usual rule is **at least 5 years after the 31 January deadline** for the relevant tax year. Always check the current page rather than relying on memory:

- [What records to keep](https://www.gov.uk/self-employed-records/what-records-to-keep)
- [How long to keep your records](https://www.gov.uk/self-employed-records/how-long-to-keep-your-records)
- [Business records overview](https://www.gov.uk/self-employed-records)

## SA103 in plain English

When you report self-employment on Self Assessment, HMRC needs a clear picture of:

- **Income** — money your business brought in
- **Expenses** — allowable costs of running the business
- **Capital / assets** — bigger items you buy and keep for the business (how you claim these depends on your accounting method; check GOV.UK)

Your sheet should make those totals easy to add up for the UK tax year (**6 April to 5 April**).

A practical way to think about columns:

| What you log in the sheet | What it helps with on SA103 |
| --- | --- |
| Income lines (date, source, amount) | Total turnover / sales |
| Expense lines (category, amount, date) | Allowable expenses by type |
| Notes / invoice refs | Finding proof if HMRC asks |
| Tax year tag (e.g. 2025/26) | Keeping years separate |

You still decide how to classify each cost under HMRC’s rules. The spreadsheet organises the evidence; it does not replace reading the guidance or getting advice on edge cases.

From the 2024 to 2025 tax year, cash basis is the default accounting method for many sole traders (you can opt out in some cases). That choice affects *when* you record income and expenses. See [Business records overview](https://www.gov.uk/self-employed-records) for the official explanation.

## Why US or generic budget templates fail UK side hustlers

A lot of free templates are built for US 1099 freelancers or for household budgeting. They often:

- Use a calendar year instead of the UK tax year (6 April–5 April)
- Use USD labels and US expense categories
- Have no link to Self Assessment / SA103 language
- Assume Excel desktop files, which is awkward if you work in the browser

A UK side-hustle tracker should speak GBP, UK tax years, and “income / expenses for Self Assessment,” not “monthly budget vs goals.”

## Minimum columns for a side-hustle sheet

You do not need a complex accounting system to start. These columns cover most sole-trader side hustles:

1. **Date** — when the money moved (or when you invoiced, depending on your method)
2. **Type** — Income or Expense
3. **Source / client / platform** — e.g. Etsy, Stripe, Client A, tutoring app
4. **Description** — short plain note
5. **Category** — e.g. platform fees, software, materials, travel
6. **Amount (GBP)** — always in pounds
7. **Tax year** — e.g. 2025/26 or 2026/27
8. **Notes / invoice number** (optional) — link back to proof

Optional extras that help later: payment method, bank account used, mileage miles (if you track travel that way), and a simple “reconciled?” flag.

Keep income and expenses in one ledger or on two tabs — either works, as long as you can filter by tax year and total cleanly.

## A simple monthly rhythm (so January is calm)

Year-end panic usually comes from empty rows, not hard tax law.

A workable rhythm:

- **Weekly (about 15 minutes):** log every payout and receipt while you still remember what it was
- **Monthly:** reconcile the sheet to bank, Stripe, PayPal, or Etsy payment statements
- **Quarterly:** skim categories for obvious mistakes (personal spend mixed in, missing fees)
- **Before you file:** export or filter the tax year, total income and expenses, and check odd items against receipts

Cash in the bank is not the same as taxable profit. Your sheet’s job is a complete list of business money in and out for the year.

## Common expense categories for UK side hustles

GOV.UK lists types of costs that may be allowable if they are for the business — for example office costs, travel, stock/materials, marketing, and some premises costs. See:

- [Expenses if you're self-employed](https://www.gov.uk/expenses-if-youre-self-employed)

Typical side-hustle categories people track (examples only — check whether *your* costs qualify):

- Platform and payment fees (Etsy, Stripe, PayPal)
- Materials and stock you sell on
- Software and subscriptions used for the business
- Advertising and website costs
- Business travel and parking (not ordinary commuting — check the rules)
- Phone / internet (business proportion only, if mixed use)
- Home office costs (business proportion or simplified expenses where allowed — verify on GOV.UK)

Rules on mileage, working from home, and mixed-use costs are detailed and change. Do not copy someone else’s percentages. Read the current GOV.UK pages or ask an adviser.

**Trading allowance:** GOV.UK explains a £1,000 trading allowance. You generally cannot claim that allowance *and* deduct actual expenses for the same income in the same way — check which option is better for you on the official guidance.

## FAQ

### Do I need Self Assessment for a small side hustle?

Often yes if you are self-employed as a sole trader and your income is above the threshold GOV.UK sets for that year (currently described as more than £1,000 before reliefs), or if another Self Assessment rule applies. Check [who must send a tax return](https://www.gov.uk/self-assessment-tax-returns/who-must-send-a-tax-return) for your situation.

### Can I use Google Sheets for HMRC records?

Yes — HMRC allows digital records if they are accurate and you can produce them if asked. Google Sheets can be a valid way to keep the working totals; keep backups and keep the underlying invoices and bank evidence too. See [self-employed records](https://www.gov.uk/self-employed-records).

### What’s the difference between a budget spreadsheet and a Self Assessment tracker?

A budget spreadsheet plans spending. A Self Assessment tracker logs actual business income and expenses by UK tax year so you can complete SA103 (and keep evidence). Categories and tax-year tags matter more than “left to spend this month.”
