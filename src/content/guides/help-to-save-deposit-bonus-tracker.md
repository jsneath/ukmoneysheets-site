## Who this is for

This is for UK savers who may be eligible for **Help to Save** — a government-backed account that pays **50p for every £1** you save, over up to **4 years** — and who want a calm monthly log in Google Sheets.

Eligibility is defined on GOV.UK, not in this article. If you are unsure, use the official checker and apply only if you qualify:

- [How Help to Save works](https://www.gov.uk/get-help-savings-low-income)
- [Eligibility](https://www.gov.uk/get-help-savings-low-income/eligibility)

This is **not** the same as a Christmas gift list or a 52-week challenge. Those are habit tools. Help to Save is a specific HMRC scheme with deposit caps and bonus timing.

## How Help to Save works in plain English

From the live GOV.UK “How it works” page (re-check before you rely on figures):

| Feature | Live GOV.UK summary |
| --- | --- |
| Bonus rate | **50p for every £1** saved (50%) |
| Monthly deposits | **£1 to £50** per **calendar month** |
| Miss a month? | You do **not** have to pay in every month |
| Cap per month | Max **£50** even if you pay several times |
| Account length | Up to **4 years**, then it closes |
| Bonuses | End of **year 2** and **year 4** |
| Withdrawals | Allowed to your bank account, but **may affect** the bonus |
| Close early | You miss the next bonus and **cannot** open another Help to Save account |

HMRC’s 21 September 2026 news frames the maximum as saving up to **£2,400** over 4 years → up to **£1,200** government bonus (paid to your **bank account**, not into the Help to Save pot). Treat that as HMRC’s framing and confirm in your account / on GOV.UK.

**Bonus maths (from HMRC “Further Information” — verify in your account):**

- **After 2 years:** first bonus = **50% of the highest balance saved** in that first period  
- **After 4 years:** final bonus = **50% of the difference** between the highest balance in years 1–2 and the highest balance in years 3–4; if the highest balance does not increase, **no final bonus**

Withdrawals and missed months can change those highest-balance figures. Your HMRC Help to Save account is the official scoreboard; a spreadsheet is only a habit log and estimate.

## Eligibility in one screen

As of the live [eligibility page](https://www.gov.uk/get-help-savings-low-income/eligibility):

- You are receiving **Universal Credit**, and  
- You (with your partner if it is a joint claim) had **take-home pay of £1 or more** in your last monthly assessment period, and  
- You live in the UK (with limited Crown / armed forces overseas exceptions)

Couples on a joint Universal Credit claim can each apply for their **own** account — separately.

If you stop claiming benefits, GOV.UK says you can **keep using** the account.

Apply via [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/get-help-savings-low-income) or the **HMRC app** — follow the official steps; do not invent forms from this article.

## Sheet columns for a Google Sheets tracker

Suggested monthly log:

1. **Month** (e.g. Oct 2026)
2. **Deposit (£)** — £0 if you skipped
3. **Running deposits (£)**
4. **Estimated balance notes** — optional; mark if you withdrew
5. **Highest balance this half** — optional helper for bonus estimates
6. **Projected year-2 bonus (£)** — **estimate only**
7. **Projected year-4 bonus (£)** — **estimate only**
8. **Withdrawn? (Y/N)**
9. **Notes**

Label every bonus column **estimate**. Recalculate after any withdrawal. Prefer the HMRC app’s bonus view when it differs from your sheet.

## Worked mini-example (fictional only)

**Example only — not a promise of your bonus.**

Sam is eligible and aims for **£50** most months.

| Period | What Sam does | Illustrative note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Months 1–24 | £50 × 24 = **£1,200** paid in, no withdrawals | First bonus estimate at 50% of highest balance — if highest balance were £1,200, a **£600** first bonus would match HMRC’s “50% of highest balance” framing (**check your account**) |
| Months 25–48 | Continues £50/month where possible | Final bonus depends on whether the **highest balance in years 3–4** rises above years 1–2 — per HMRC’s difference rule |

If Sam withdraws heavily in year 3, the final bonus can shrink or disappear even if total paid-in looks large. That is why the sheet tracks withdrawals, not only deposits.

## Habit tips

- Set a standing order for an amount you can afford (**£1–£50**)
- Missing a month is fine — pay what you can next calendar month
- Do not close the account early if you still want the next bonus (per GOV.UK)
- Keep an emergency buffer elsewhere so you are less tempted to drain Help to Save

## FAQ

### Who can open a Help to Save account?
Follow [GOV.UK eligibility](https://www.gov.uk/get-help-savings-low-income/eligibility). At time of writing it centres on Universal Credit with take-home pay of £1+ in the last assessment period (plus UK residence rules).

### How much can I pay in each month?
Between **£1 and £50** per calendar month — [How it works](https://www.gov.uk/get-help-savings-low-income).

### When do I get the Help to Save bonus?
At the end of the **second** and **fourth** years, based on how much you have saved — paid to your **bank account**.

### How is the 2-year vs 4-year bonus calculated?
HMRC’s 21 Sep 2026 further information: year-2 bonus = 50% of highest balance in the first period; year-4 bonus = 50% of the rise in highest balance between the first two years and the last two years (no rise → no final bonus). Verify in your account / on GOV.UK.

### What if I withdraw money or miss a month?
Missing a month is allowed. Withdrawals are allowed but may affect the bonus. Closing early means missing the next bonus and you cannot open another account — [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/get-help-savings-low-income).

### Has Help to Save been made permanent / what’s changing in 2028?
HMRC’s 21 Sep 2026 news says the scheme is now permanent and eligibility expands from 2028. Check GOV.UK nearer the time for live detail.

### Is this financial advice?
No.
